# Writing good settings

LinkPilot's AI only knows what you tell it. A small, fast model such as `gpt-oss-20b` follows clear, specific input very well and vague input badly. Spend ten minutes here and every message improves.

## Offer

Two or three specific sentences: **who you help, with what, and the outcome.** No slogans.

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| "We provide innovative digital solutions to grow your business." | "We build WooCommerce stores and order-alert automations for small online retailers in Bangladesh. Most projects go live in two weeks." |
| "AI-powered next-generation platform." | "We automate quote follow-ups for small logistics teams, so no enquiry sits unanswered for more than an hour." |

## Proof points

The AI is told it may cite **only** your offer and your proof points. One fact per line, true and checkable.

```
Built the order-alert system now used by 40+ WooCommerce stores
Cut quote follow-up time by 70% for Harbor Tech (logistics, 35 staff)
Agency founded 2015, clients in Bangladesh and the UK
```

- Do not write anything you could not defend if the prospect asked. The AI will repeat it.
- If you have no proof points yet, leave the field empty. Messages then stay general instead of inventing results.

## Audience

Who you want to reach, in one line. The AI uses it to judge relevance and angle.

```
Operations and growth leads at 10 to 200 person logistics and retail companies
```

## Tone

The default is short and safe: `Friendly, concise, professional. No hype, no emojis, no buzzwords.`

Useful variations:

- `Direct and a little informal, like a founder writing to another founder. Two sentences maximum.`
- `Polite and formal. Use full sentences and no contractions.`

Avoid asking for "persuasive" or "compelling" copy. That pushes models toward exactly the clichés LinkPilot tries to remove.

## Goal

One outcome per sequence. Default: `Book a 15-minute intro call`. Alternatives: `Get a reply about their current process`, `Invite them to a free audit`.

## Calendar link

Shared only in **replies**, and only when the prospect is interested or asks to talk. Never in notes, openers or follow-ups.

## Objection notes (optional)

Short rules for common pushback. The AI uses them when a reply raises the objection.

```
Too expensive -> mention the free 20-minute audit first
We already have an agency -> say we often work alongside an existing team
Not now -> thank them, ask if it is fine to check in next quarter
```

## What the AI will not do, whatever you write

- Invent facts, results, customers, mutual connections or shared history.
- Include emojis, markdown, placeholders such as `[Name]`, em dashes, or any link except your calendar link (in replies).
- Deny being automated when asked. Questions about bots, pricing, contracts, legal issues, or an upset tone are always handed to you.

## Check the output before you scale

1. Turn approval mode ON and run 5 to 10 leads.
2. Read every draft. If a draft is generic, the cause is almost always a vague offer or missing proof point, so fix the setting rather than editing each draft.
3. Use **Regenerate** a few times to see the range. If you see a phrase you dislike, add a line to **Tone**, for example `Never say "reaching out".`
